About Additive Edge

What does Additive Edge do?

Additive Edge is an on-demand 3D printing service based in Sharon, Massachusetts. We produce polymer, metal, ceramic and composite parts, plus printed PCBs, in-house and through a vetted network of manufacturing partners. You work with one team that handles the quote, the print and the quality check.

Where is Additive Edge located, and what areas do you serve?

Additive Edge is based in Sharon, MA 02067, south of Boston. We serve customers across Massachusetts and New England directly, and customers worldwide through our partner network.

Is Additive Edge a 3D printing shop or a broker?

Both. We print in-house for day-to-day work, with full control over quality and timeline, and we extend that with a partner network for specialized equipment, exotic materials and extra capacity. Either way you have one point of contact, one quote and one invoice.

Why use a 3D printing network instead of a single print shop?

A single shop can only offer the machines it owns. Through Additive Edge you get access to 100+ machine platforms and 45+ materials, so the process is chosen to fit your part and not the other way around. The network also adds surge capacity when a deadline is tight.

What kinds of parts do you make?

We make functional prototypes, end-use production parts, jigs, fixtures and tooling, tight-tolerance parts, low-volume runs from tens to thousands, and bridge production while hard tooling is being built.

Do you only make prototypes, or production parts too?

Both. Additive Edge produces test-ready prototypes and end-use components built to ship in your product, including low-volume and bridge production runs.

How long has Additive Edge been in 3D printing?

Our team has more than 10 years of experience in additive manufacturing, on both the customer side and the equipment manufacturer side. Read more about Additive Edge.

Who manages quality when a partner prints my part?

We do. Additive Edge applies one quality standard across every partner and coordinates sourcing, production, finishing and inspection, so you receive finished parts from a single accountable team.

Processes and materials

What 3D printing processes do you offer?

Additive Edge offers FDM/FFF, SLA, DLP and LCD resin printing, SLS, MJF and material jetting, binder jetting, metal laser powder bed fusion (LPBF/DMLS), bound metal deposition (BMD) and wire-arc additive manufacturing (WAAM). See the full list on our capabilities page.

Do you offer metal 3D printing?

Yes. We print metal parts by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF/DMLS), binder jetting, bound metal deposition and wire-arc additive manufacturing. Available metals include titanium, 316L and 17-4 stainless steel, H13 tool steel, copper, aluminum, Inconel 718 and Inconel 625.

What materials can you 3D print?

We print in 45+ materials across five families: metals, polymers, composites, ceramics and specialty materials. That includes PEEK, PEKK, Ultem, nylon (PA6, PA12), TPU, carbon-fiber and glass-fiber composites, zirconia, alumina, silicone and foam. If you don't see the material you need, ask and we will help you find it.

Can you 3D print PEEK, PEKK or Ultem?

Yes. Additive Edge prints high-temperature polymers including PEEK, PEKK and Ultem, as well as carbon-fiber and glass-fiber filled versions of PEEK and Ultem.

Can you 3D print ceramic parts?

Yes. We print zirconia and alumina, which offer high-temperature stability, abrasion resistance and electrical insulation. They are used for tooling, wear parts and precision electrical components.

Can you 3D print flexible or rubber-like parts?

Yes. We print flexible parts in TPU, silicone and foam materials.

Can you 3D print circuit boards or electronics?

Yes. Additive Edge produces printed PCBs and additively manufactured electronics, and supplies Ataru, a high-temperature resin with low dielectric loss for RF and electronics work.

What is the difference between SLS and MJF?

Both are powder-based polymer processes that need no support structures. The main difference is the energy source: SLS fuses the powder with a laser, while MJF uses a fusing agent and infrared heat. SLS produces durable, isotropic nylon-class parts. MJF offers fine feature fidelity and a strong surface finish for assembly-ready parts. Tell us about your part and we will recommend one.

Can you reverse engineer or 3D scan a part without a CAD file?

Yes. Additive Edge can 3D scan and reverse engineer a part when no CAD file exists. Describe the part in the quote form to get started.

Do you help with design for additive manufacturing (DfAM)?

Yes. We provide design for additive manufacturing support to make parts printable, stronger and cheaper to produce.

Do you offer CNC machining for printed parts?

Yes. Through our machine shop partnership we have access to 16 CNC machines for precision machining, hybrid workflows and metal finishing.

What finishing options do you offer?

We offer machining, surface finishing, threading, assembly and painting, so parts ship ready to use.

Do you inspect parts before they ship?

Yes. Parts are verified in a dedicated metrology and inspection lab for tolerances, material properties and part quality before delivery.

Rizium GF and CF filament

What is Rizium filament?

Rizium is an engineering 3D printing filament based on a cyclic olefin copolymer (COC) blend reinforced with glass fiber (Rizium GF) or carbon fiber (Rizium CF). It prints rigid, near-isotropic parts with a clean surface finish and absorbs almost no moisture, so it does not need drying.

Does Rizium filament need to be dried before printing?

No. Rizium absorbs less than 0.01% moisture, so it is ready to print straight from the spool with no drying.

What is the difference between Rizium GF and Rizium CF?

Rizium GF is glass-fiber reinforced and Rizium CF is carbon-fiber reinforced. CF is the stiffer grade, with a tensile modulus of 6.7 GPa against 3.6 GPa for GF, and prints in matte black. GF has slightly higher impact strength. Both share the same no-drying COC base.

Is Rizium a good alternative to nylon or carbon-fiber nylon?

For many parts, yes. Rizium has properties similar to nylon and comparable composites but is much easier to print: no warping and no drying. Its limits are heat and fuel. The heat deflection temperature is about 75°C, and it is not suited to hydrocarbon exposure.

What printer do I need for Rizium GF or Rizium CF?

Any FFF printer with hardened gears and a hardened nozzle. Rizium is 1.75 mm filament and prints with a nozzle at 220 to 260°C and a bed at 90°C.

Does Rizium need an enclosure, and which bed surface works best?

No enclosure is needed. A rougher, textured build plate works best for Rizium.

What is Rizium filament best used for?

Rizium GF suits functional prototypes, end-use parts, jigs and fixtures, drone and UAV frames, and cleanroom or lab parts. Rizium CF suits gears, structural brackets, robotic end-effectors, drone parts and tooling where stiffness matters.

Is Rizium good for cold or outdoor environments?

Yes. Rizium holds its mechanical properties in sub-zero conditions and is UV stable, which makes it a good fit for drones and other parts used outdoors in the cold.

When should I not use Rizium?

Avoid Rizium for automotive parts exposed to hydrocarbons such as fuel and oil, and for sustained use above its heat deflection temperature of about 75°C (GF) or 73°C (CF).

Is Rizium safe to print indoors?

Rizium is a low-VOC material and does not require venting. Rizium GF and the COC base used in Rizium CF are GREENGUARD certified.

What is Rizium Support?

Rizium Support is a copolyester breakaway support filament made for the Rizium line. It is compatible with every Rizium filament, breaks away cleanly and is not water-soluble.

Where can I buy Rizium filament?

Additive Edge sells Rizium GF and Rizium CF in 500 g and 820 g spools, from $99, and Rizium Support in 500 g spools, in our online shop.

Can you print my part in Rizium for me?

Yes. Send the part through the quote form and Additive Edge will produce it in Rizium GF or Rizium CF.

Ataru high-temp resin

What is Ataru resin?

Ataru is a UV-curable high-temperature resin for SLA, DLP, LCD and MSLA printers. It has a heat deflection temperature above 300°C, high tensile strength and low dielectric loss.

What 3D printing resin can withstand 300°C?

Ataru high-temp resin has a heat deflection temperature above 300°C (ISO 75), which makes it suitable for high-temperature tooling, mold inserts and electronics. Additive Edge sells it in 1 L and 5 L volumes.

What post-cure does Ataru resin need?

Bake Ataru parts in an oven at 200°C for 2 hours to post-cure them.

What is Ataru resin used for?

Ataru is used for high-temperature tooling and molds, RF and antenna components, additively manufactured electronics and functional prototypes where heat resistance, electrical stability and surface finish matter.

Is Ataru resin good for RF and high-frequency parts?

Yes. Ataru has low dielectric loss and a dielectric constant of about 3.1 at 1 MHz, which suits RF and high-frequency components.

Which printers work with Ataru resin?

Ataru is a low-viscosity resin compatible with SLA, DLP, LCD and MSLA resin printers.

What are the mechanical properties of Ataru resin?

Cured Ataru has a tensile strength of 65 MPa, a tensile modulus of 2.7 GPa, a flexural strength of 80 MPa and 5% elongation at break. These values are indicative.

What is the shelf life of Ataru resin?

Ataru resin has a shelf life of about 2 years.

How do I clean Ataru prints?

Use Ataru Resin Cleaner, a post-print wash formulated for Ataru and similar high-temperature photopolymers. It is available in 1 L and 5 L containers.

When should I not use Ataru resin?

Ataru is not recommended for long-term hydrocarbon exposure.
